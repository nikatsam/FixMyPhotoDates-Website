Organize Photos From Multiple Sources: Fix Date Conflicts

You got photos from everywhere: iPhone, Android, cloud storage, USB drive, old laptop backup, your camera. Now you're combining them into one library. But each source has different dates. Some are missing dates entirely. Your unified library is chaos—photos out of order, conflicts, duplicates.

The Problem

Different devices and services store photo dates differently:

  • Phone and camera photos: May contain an embedded EXIF capture date
  • Google Takeout: Can include JSON sidecars with photo date information
  • Copied files: Filesystem timestamps may differ from the capture date
  • Older archives: Some photos may have missing metadata or date-bearing filenames

Different date fields can disagree, and some files may have no clear capture date. Check which field your photo app uses before deciding what to change.

Typical Scenario: The Chaos

This story is common:

  • A folder contains photos from different phones and cameras
  • Some files have embedded capture dates; others only have filesystem timestamps
  • A Google Takeout export includes JSON sidecars for some images
  • Filename patterns provide clues for a subset of the photos
  • You want to inspect those dates before importing the files into another app

Understanding Your Photo Sources

📱 iPhone / iOS

May contain an EXIF capture date. Check the camera clock and actual date before using it as a repair target.

🤖 Android Phones

Good EXIF data, but sometimes stored in different location. Scan all metadata fields.

☁️ Google Photos Export

May include JSON sidecars with photoTakenTime. The app can match supported sidecars when enabled.

🔗 OneDrive / Dropbox Sync

Behavior varies by service and settings. Inspect downloaded files and compare their available date fields.

💾 USB Drive / External Backup

Filesystem timestamps can reflect a later copy. Embedded metadata and filenames may still provide useful clues.

📷 Camera / DSLR

Usually has accurate dates if camera clock was set correctly. Trust these dates.

How to Resolve Conflicts

When a photo has 3 different dates, which one is correct?

Smart Priority Order

  • Embedded photo date: EXIF capture date is checked first when present
  • Google Takeout sidecar: Checked when enabled and no embedded date is found
  • Filename date: Supported patterns can provide a proposed date
  • Other clues: Chronology, optional OCR, and manual review may help when dates are missing

The app follows a defined extraction order and shows the source and confidence for its proposed date. Review uncertain or surprising results before applying them.

Handling Duplicates

Fix My Photo Dates repairs dates; it does not identify or remove duplicate photos. Review duplicates separately before or after date repair.

  • Use a dedicated duplicate finder to compare identical or similar images.
  • Keep an independent backup before deleting or moving duplicate files.
  • Use this app to inspect and repair dates on the files you choose.

FAQ

Can you detect duplicate photos?

No. The app does not identify or remove duplicate photos. Use a dedicated duplicate-management tool for that task.

What if a photo has no EXIF data?

The app checks Google Takeout JSON sidecars when enabled, supported filename patterns, chronological context, and optional timestamp OCR. If no reliable date is available, the file is marked uncertain for manual review.

Can I trust the app to pick the right date?

Review the detected date, source, and confidence before applying a repair. You can exclude a file or manually override a suggested date.

What about cloud-stored photos?

Our app works with local files. Download them first (Google Takeout, OneDrive sync, etc.), then let our app fix the dates. We can't access cloud APIs directly—only local files.
