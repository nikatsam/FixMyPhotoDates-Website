Fix Photo Dates After Moving Files: Restore Your Timeline

You organized your photo folders. Or you copied files to a new drive. Or you cleaned up your library. Now when you sort by date, everything is wrong. All your photos show today's date instead of when you took them. Your timeline is destroyed.

The Problem

Copying a photo can give it a new filesystem "Date created" even when its embedded "Date taken" (EXIF) is still intact. The dates describe different things, and photo apps may sort using embedded capture dates or filesystem timestamps depending on the app and view.

Why it hurts: Your photo library displays chronologically by the wrong date. Albums are scrambled. Years of photos look like they were all taken yesterday.

Why Windows Does This

It's not intentional—it's how file systems work:

  • Copy operation creates a new filesystem entry: The destination file's "Date created" may reflect when it was copied.
  • Move behavior varies: Moving within a drive and moving across drives can affect filesystem timestamps differently.
  • Filesystem dates are not capture dates: "Date created" and "Date modified" describe the file on disk, not necessarily when the photo was taken.
  • Photo apps choose their own sort field: Check the date field and sorting behavior in the app where you view the photos.
  • EXIF data gets lost in some scenarios: If you use certain photo tools or batch editors, the original EXIF might get stripped

How to Diagnose: Is This Actually Your Problem?

1

Check File Properties

Right-click a photo → Properties. Look at "Date modified". Does it show today's date or the date you organized?

2

Check EXIF Metadata

Right-click photo → Properties → Details tab. Look for "Date taken". Is it different from "Date modified"?

3

View in Photo App

Open photos in Windows Photos or similar. Are they in random order? All showing same date?

4

Remember When

Do you remember approximately when you moved these files? If all photos show that date, your EXIF is intact but file dates got overwritten.

If "Date created" is recent but the photo was taken years ago, compare it with the embedded "Date taken" before changing anything.

The Good News: EXIF Data Probably Still Exists

Moving files often leaves embedded EXIF metadata intact, which may provide a reliable capture date:

  • ✓ EXIF "Date Taken" is still inside the file
  • ✓ Filesystem creation and modified dates are separate values
  • ✓ A capture date may be recoverable from embedded metadata
  • ✓ Review the evidence before choosing which date fields to update

The appropriate repair depends on the app you use: update filesystem timestamps, embedded EXIF dates, or both after reviewing the preview.

Prevention: How to Avoid This In Future

  • Use photo management software: Lightroom, Photos app, Capture One preserve EXIF dates during moves
  • Avoid Windows file operations: Don't use File Explorer to move photos. Use photo software instead.
  • Check before organizing: Before you move 10,000 photos, test with 10. See if dates survive.
  • Backup originals: Keep original files in case dates do get corrupted
  • Fix dates first: If you accidentally moved files, fix dates before doing more organizing
