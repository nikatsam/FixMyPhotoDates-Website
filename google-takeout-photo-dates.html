Fix Google Takeout Photo Dates: Restore Your Correct Timeline

You downloaded your photos from Google Photos. But when you opened them, they're all showing the download date instead of when you actually took them. Your photo library is now in random order. Your albums are scrambled. This is fixable—instantly.

The Problem

When you download photos from Google Takeout, Google doesn't include the original photo date in the default export. Instead, your files get the current download date as their metadata. So a photo you took in 2015 now shows as "2024" because that's when you downloaded it.

Result: Your photo library displays chronologically by download order, not by actual photo date. Albums show photos randomly. Your timeline is destroyed.

Why Does Google Takeout Do This?

It's a technical limitation, not intentional:

  • Google Photos stores dates separately: The photo's actual "Date Taken" lives in EXIF metadata and Google's backend database, not in the exported file
  • Takeout export doesn't include EXIF by default: You get the image file, but the creation date metadata is lost
  • Windows uses "Date Modified": When Takeout exports files, they get the current download date as modification time
  • Results matter more than metadata to Google: Google Photos shows correct dates in their app, so they assume users don't care about file-level metadata

How to Diagnose: Do You Actually Have This Problem?

Before fixing, confirm this is what happened:

1

Open File Properties

Right-click a photo from your Takeout download → Properties → check "Date modified"

2

Check Against Original

Does the date match when you downloaded? Or does it match the real photo date? (If you remember when you took it)

3

View in Photo App

Open photos in Windows Photos or similar. Do they show in correct chronological order? If random, dates are broken.

4

Check JSON Sidecar Files

Google Takeout includes .json files with each photo. These contain the REAL dates. So the metadata exists—just in a separate file.

If all your Takeout photos show the same date (your download date), you have this issue.

Can You Manually Fix This? (The Hard Way)

Technically yes, but it's painful:

  • ✗ Extract dates from JSON sidecar files manually
  • ✗ Match them to corresponding image files
  • ✗ Use command-line tools to update file metadata
  • ✗ Handle conflicts when dates don't match
  • ✗ Risk corrupting files if you make mistakes
  • ✗ Repeat for thousands of photos

Most people quit after 20 photos.

Why This Happens to Other Cloud Services Too

Takeout isn't unique:

  • Other services: Downloaded files can have filesystem dates that differ from the capture date
  • Metadata varies: Export services preserve photo dates differently; inspect files and accompanying metadata

The app can inspect local image files using supported embedded metadata and filename patterns. Google Takeout JSON sidecar matching is its specific sidecar workflow.

Prevention: How to Avoid This Next Time

For future Google Takeout exports:

  • Keep the JSON files: Google includes them for a reason. Don't delete them.
  • Don't re-organize immediately: Moving files before fixing dates makes it harder to match files to metadata.
  • Use structured export: When possible, keep photos in the folder structure Google provides.
  • Fix dates before organizing: Let our tool restore dates first, then reorganize by date safely.
